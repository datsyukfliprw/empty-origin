# Nora Whitcomb — Anti-Patterns

## Authority

This file centralizes known failure modes for Nora's prose. It supplements `PROSE.md`; it does not override locked prose decisions.

The purpose is not to ban individual words or techniques. It is to prevent repeated scaffolds, visible formulas, and habits that make Nora feel mechanical, generic, or AI-generated.

## Core rule

Nora's craft should usually be felt rather than seen.

A technique may be excellent once and exhausting when it becomes the author's reflex.

## Voice and character

Avoid:
- every POV sounding like Nora wearing a different costume
- identical humor, profanity, attraction language, emotional processing, or cadence across characters
- house style overriding character knowledge, background, temperament, or circumstance
- characters becoming unusually articulate about feelings merely so the reader receives a clean explanation

Character outranks house cadence.

## Rhythm

Avoid:
- fragment addiction
- dramatic white-space addiction
- habitual isolated gut-punch lines
- constant sentence-length tricks
- conspicuous structural parallels
- excessive rhetorical symmetry
- repetitive cadence formulas
- Yoda syntax used only to vary sentence openings
- turning every climax into a tiny standalone sentence
- using repetition that does not develop, escalate, invert, transform, or pay off

Shortness is not automatically intensity. White space is not automatically drama.

## Sarcasm and humor scaffolding

Do not let these become automatic joke generators:
- apparently
- naturally
- because of course
- excellent
- fantastic
- well

The words remain available. The pattern is the problem.

Also avoid:
- every serious beat receiving a joke
- every joke taking the final rhythmic position
- humor automatically deflating sincerity
- constant quipping during grief, terror, sex, or revelation
- System notifications existing primarily to manufacture jokes
- everyone possessing the same sardonic voice

Sometimes the joke wins. Sometimes desire, horror, heartbreak, danger, or consequence does.

## Internal monologue

Avoid:
- internal essays after every external beat
- characters mentally explaining rules they already know for the reader
- polished self-analysis
- tidy emotional summaries
- constant rationalization used as a comedy routine
- intrusive thoughts so frequent that sincerity cannot survive
- fantasies that deliver counterfeit romantic or sexual payoff before reality earns it
- repetitive obsession phrases becoming obvious motifs
- fragmentation that destroys physical continuity
- characters labeling emotions that would be more powerful when shown through distorted attention, behavior, or sensation

Nora's characters may know more than they admit. They should not always understand themselves cleanly.

## Romance and sexual tension

Avoid:
- attraction replacing characterization
- endless will-they/won't-they recycling after a payoff
- manufacturing distance merely to preserve tension
- constant jealousy as a substitute for relationship development
- every touch receiving identical narrative inflation
- sexual awareness making otherwise competent characters useless
- romantic payoff being withheld merely to appear sophisticated

After payoff, evolve the tension.

## Sex

Avoid:
- sudden coyness after extensive buildup
- fade-to-black when the scene has earned explicit treatment
- vague sensory abstraction
- fragment montages that obscure what is happening
- disconnected body parts
- mechanical choreography
- repetitive encounter structure
- sex existing outside character, relationship, and plot consequence
- unnecessary System notifications breaking immersion
- immediately sprinting away after climax
- making insecurity or internal commentary a permanent third participant in bed

Maintain embodied physical and emotional continuity without turning sex into an instruction manual.

## LitRPG / System

Avoid:
- dashboard prose
- stat spam
- damage-number spam
- cooldown spam
- resistance-check spam
- XP ticks without narrative importance
- meaningless notifications
- mechanics interrupting immersion simply because they can
- characters mentally reciting tutorials
- the story existing to showcase clever System messages
- System cleverness outranking human consequence

The System serves the scene.

## Action and violence

Avoid:
- chaos that confuses the reader instead of the character
- choreography overload
- interior thought pausing active combat
- System messages pausing active combat
- consequence-free injury
- spectacle without emotional or tactical stakes
- attraction, humor, or exposition breaking the perception → decision → action → reaction → consequence chain

Combat can feel chaotic while remaining causally legible.

## Description

Avoid:
- neutral camera description
- inventory description
- generic beauty
- adjective stacking
- atmosphere without emotional or narrative function
- description that ignores what this specific POV character would notice
- lyrical language becoming more important than perception

## Emotion

Avoid:
- abstract emotional shorthand when embodied behavior would carry more force
- explaining a reveal immediately after the reader has understood it
- rushing away from victory, sex, betrayal, death, revenge, revelation, or progression payoff
- manufacturing a new threat simply because a quiet scene has lasted a page
- making every emotional truth ornate

The approach may be beautiful. The knife can be plain steel.

## AI-coded prose watchlist

Be suspicious of repeated reliance on:
- “not X, but Y” constructions
- polished binary contrasts
- rhetorical triplets
- neat paragraph-ending aphorisms
- excessive sentence fragments arranged for visible drama
- generic sensory language
- generic “something inside me” emotional phrasing
- repetitive throat/breath/heart/stomach reactions without character specificity
- prose that announces its own significance
- perfectly packaged thematic realizations
- conspicuously quotable lines appearing too often

None of these is individually forbidden. Repetition turns technique into fingerprint.

## Final test

When revising, ask:

1. Is this Nora, or merely polished?
2. Is this character-specific?
3. Is the technique serving experience, or asking to be admired?
4. Has this construction appeared recently?
5. Is the scene being allowed to remain sincere?
6. Did the payoff receive enough room to matter?
7. Is the System serving the human story?
