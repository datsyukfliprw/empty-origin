# Maya — ongoing series bible

## Authority and scope

The author has explicitly chosen a **longer ongoing series** and established that **Maya never returns to Earth. This world is her new life.** These are binding directions. A trilogy, an eventual homecoming, or repeated near-homecomings would contradict them.

Everything below that describes unwritten events is an editorial plan, not manuscript canon. The chapter files govern what has happened. **Book One, *Empty Origin*, is complete through Chapter Thirty-Two at 97,048 whitespace-counted words in the September 24 active working revision.** Read [project notes](book1/PROJECT_NOTES.md), the [chapter ledger](book1/CHAPTER_LEDGER.md), and the [publication roadmap](book1/PUBLICATION_ROADMAP.md) for the exact current state. The [Book One completion plan](book1/BOOK_ONE_PLAN.md) is now a historical planning record rather than an unwritten horizon.

## The promise that can sustain the series

A stranded nineteen-year-old gamer learns to hold her ground in a world that can see her level before it knows her name. As she becomes a Warder, she builds a company, falls in love, and opens dangerous routes through a world she must learn to call home.

The recurring pleasure is watching Maya become capable: a person who once needed help crossing a town gate eventually helps decide which routes her people can survive. Her attention, humor, stubbornness, growing combat skills, and sometimes unwise attachments should make that progression specific to her.

The durable story mechanism is a small, independent expedition company working around damaged gates and the places they connect. Jobs can involve rescue, escort, recovery, exploration, monster threats, or protecting an established settlement. Each job has a concrete person or place at stake, opposition with something to gain, and a decision Maya cannot outsource. Rewards buy equipment, instruction, freedom of movement, and a home that can accumulate history.

Introduce the company through people choosing and paying for their first shared undertaking. A founding charter can be summarized. Readers need to see who turns up, who owns the gear, who disagrees, and whether they can make the job work.

**Positioning and governing engine:** **LitRPG plot + romantasy emotional engine + explicit payoff.** The source of serial momentum is Maya's life here: progression, dungeon-scale expeditions, routes, rescue, party tactics, gear, system discovery, and the relationships that increasingly shape her choices. Do not advertise a conventional exclusive-couple romance or a settled multiple-partner structure before the manuscript delivers that promise. Earned sexual encounters stay on page. Preserve explicit physical continuity, active consent, character-specific desire, and the emotional aftermath instead of fading after the decision is made.

## Dungeon and expedition engine

Dungeons are now a recurring LitRPG delivery mechanism for the series. They should grow out of this world's existing infrastructure and ecology: damaged transit stations, sealed maintenance complexes, drowned works, abandoned routes, monster-held ruins, fractured gate clusters, cisterns, quarries, and other System-active sites.

A dungeon is a bounded expedition problem, not a detached video-game instance. It should have a concrete reason to enter, a governing environmental/System mechanic, party roles, escalating zones or phases, finite resources, a meaningful encounter, a progression payoff, a cost, and an exit that matters.

Use dungeons to deliver both LitRPG and relationship progression:

- Maya's build changes through levels, techniques, gear, tactical mastery, or System knowledge.
- Other party members remain indispensable.
- Competence attraction, forced proximity, watches, safe rooms, wound care, and adrenaline aftermath can deepen romance.
- Explicit intimacy belongs only in genuinely secured pauses or aftermaths and must change the relationship afterward.
- Do not make every dungeon end in sex or every dungeon reward a level.

Book One introduces one true recovery delve before the rescue, then treats Fallow Reach as the larger dungeon-scale climax. Future books should usually contain at least one substantial delve or equivalent bounded expedition when appropriate. See [book1/LITRPG_ROMANTASY_DUNGEON_PLAN.md](book1/LITRPG_ROMANTASY_DUNGEON_PLAN.md) for the detailed framework and Book One cadence.

## Permanent settlement and Earth

Maya's first wish to get home is already written and emotionally credible. Preserve it as her starting point. Over Book One she begins making commitments here that matter even when she is not thinking about Earth. By its ending, she has chosen work, people, and somewhere to return after a job. The next book's objective concerns those commitments.

Establish by the end of Book Two that her arrival cannot be reversed. Give the conclusion adequate physical and historical evidence, then stop using a possible return as a plot reward. The knowledge can hurt without undoing the future she has begun to choose.

No temporary Earth visit, bodily reset, resurrection in her apartment, simulation reveal, or final-volume homecoming. Memory and ordinary dreams can preserve her family. Do not kill or vilify her parents to make staying easier. Do not bring her whole former life across to erase the cost. A future communication scene would require a deliberate new story decision; it is not part of this plan or a substitute for her new life.

Her father taught her things she can use here. Her mother can remain present in how she folds a shirt or cares for someone. Eventually she should reach for a local person's name under pressure without announcing that this proves she has healed. She can miss Earth years after she stops trying to leave.

## Private mystery design — spoilers for unwritten books

These are the default solutions to write toward, subject to compatibility checks as scenes develop. They prevent the same mystery from being stretched indefinitely. Do not insert them into the factual ledger before they are dramatized.

### The sluice and the English room

The visible room is a receiving station within the new world's damaged transit infrastructure. Earlier arrivals adapted part of it with objects and fittings brought from Earth. English writing establishes the history of the objects; it never established the room's present location. The lit sign is real, the room is real, and Iven is real.

Book One must demonstrate this through a combination of exterior observation, a station plan that matches the explored rooms, and physical evidence of older local construction beneath imported fittings. An unfamiliar vista alone is insufficient. The group also learns what is powering the lights and why stored food and a tap remain usable. No inexhaustible pantry or working electricity without a source.

The ordinary sluice feeds a much older transit junction. The inner wheel changes the local coupling to that junction. A damaged receiving selector can drift independently of the restrained spindle; this explains why the same mark does not guarantee the same water or view. Pressure conditions matter, but the final mechanism must be defined clearly enough that a reader can anticipate a dangerous change. Do not use randomness to produce whatever the scene needs.

The shortened rope was severed at a changing boundary. A Brace can hold its support steady but cannot make that boundary remain open or protect whatever crosses it. The eventual rescue succeeds by learning a usable interval, building a sound route, and coordinating both ends. It must not repeat Elena's failed pull with stronger magic.

Iven's isolation occurred inside a damaged interval of the station. Book One confirms that elapsed time differed; Book Two establishes the limited circumstances and closes off convenient time travel. Characters cannot turn a wheel backward to revisit mistakes, restore the dead, or undo a battle. Keep a private interval table before writing exact ratios. The seventy-one sleeps remain an incomplete count until supported independently.

### Arrival and permanent loss

Maya's transport was a one-way capture event in the receiving network, not a reward for winning the raid, a game subscription, a prophecy, or a favor from a god. The game did not create this world. Other arrivals, including Elena, demonstrate that Maya is one person caught in a larger failure.

The event does not preserve a usable Earth-side endpoint. The regional gates subsequently explored connect places in her new world; controlling them cannot recreate the missing source. By the end of Book Two, independent records, physical examination, and a bounded test support that conclusion. Do not make a charismatic expert's unsupported “impossible” the entire proof.

The acute symptoms in Chapter One belong to the transport event, but characters should not diagnose the vanished Earth body as a medical case from a distance. Reveal the correspondence through documented arrivals, not a new narrator explaining her apartment. Her parents' response remains outside Maya's direct knowledge unless separately evidenced.

### System and origin

Runs grow from learned patterns; practice, judgment, cost, and embodiment remain central. The System existed locally before Earth's arrivals. It is neither Maya's game interface nor a benevolent quest dispenser.

Working explanation for translation: arrival attunes a newcomer to the receiving region's common language and script. It does not teach engineering, decrypt codes, or give local people English. Test limits in ordinary situations before using one as a plot obstacle. Do not turn Maya into the only possible translator forever: other arrivals exist and local people can learn languages.

Maya's empty origin reflects the absence of a registered affiliation here. The precise mechanics need controlled demonstration before a plot depends on them. The social consequences are already real; the blank does not confer immunity to debts, contracts, magic, or injury. No rare class or supreme administrator power simply for being from Earth.

### Elena and old characters

Elena is an active person with a life in this world, not a trail of abandoned notebooks. Find her alive in Book Two. She left Rook after its refusal to search as she wanted and its retention of her work; she continued investigating the regional network and made attachments elsewhere. Let her be mistaken about some things and accountable for choices. She should not explain everything in one lecture.

Hale's attention and Tolliver's suspended witness standing should receive a bounded answer when the story next returns to Greyward, no later than Book Two. A strong default is that Hale recognizes a pattern of vulnerable unheld arrivals from ordinary prior cases. He need not be a secret prince, creator, or late romantic option.

Ordway continues to run a precarious business. If Tolliver makes restitution, show a cost to him and give her control over whether she accepts it. Forgiveness is not a romantic reward Maya can grant on her behalf.

## Progression that keeps delivering

Current canon through Twenty-Two: Maya is level three, progress 15/18, reserve 5/5, Warder, with Brace and Stepguard. She owns a repaired green shield. Her left ankle has improved enough for flat indoor walking and limited stairs but is still restricted from field work and shield practice. The working target is a genuinely useful novice by Book One's end, provisionally around level five or six if the earned events support it. This is an editorial target, not a System rule or an instruction to award points at chapter boundaries.

Sixteen–Twenty-Two delivered the next level, owned shield, a limited mobile defensive option and the beginning of a recognizable style: create room for people to move, read pressure, coordinate the next action, release before a hold becomes a trap. The drainage route now operates, the Fallow Reach receiving station is confirmed local, and the first spanning test failed safely because the team honored abort rules. She still needs a clean win and further earned progression. Injury and fear cannot consume every reward.

| Stage | New capability | Continuing limit | A scene that proves the gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book One | Reliable Brace; a separately learned brief moving hold; use of a purchased or earned shield. | Ordinary skin, limited reserve, weak supports, angles and timing. | A safe drill establishes the moving hold, a field mistake exposes its limit, and the climax recombines both lessons. |
| Book Two | Maintain protection while a small party changes position; begin a chosen offensive complement. | Protection covers a defined direction or person, not everyone; spending competes with her own defense. | An escort succeeds because she selects who moves when; another capable person covers the gap she leaves. |
| Books Three–Four | Work with overlapping defenses and a prepared position; competent close combat. | Set-up, equipment, terrain, independent teammates, and recovery. | An opponent learns her usual formation, forcing her to win with a new arrangement. |
| Books Five–Six | Lead experienced teams through large hazards; specialize through established instruction and field work. | Distance, coordination, divided objectives, and consequences after the fight. | Delegation succeeds where personally holding one doorway would lose the wider mission. |
| Later books | Refine mastery, train others, adapt to unfamiliar combinations. | She cannot be in two places; power does not select the right obligation for her. | A student or ally solves something she would have mishandled alone. |

Write the mechanical record before drafting each new ability: source of instruction, activation, cost, duration, valid targets, failure conditions, interaction with old abilities, and visible evidence of learning. Choose exact numbers only when the scene and existing rules support them. Once shown, preserve them. Level-ups do not refill reserve or heal wounds unless a distinct established rule says so.

Regular progression rewards can include gear, knowledge, tactics, and mastery as well as levels. None should become an excuse to postpone new magical capabilities for another half-book. Maya's original desire for an exciting weapon deserves eventual payment. Learning to use a sword need not discard Warder or duplicate Nyx's build.

## People readers will want to revisit

**Maya:** She is good at seeing where a group is failing and reluctant to expose how badly she wants its approval. Being useful can become another way to let other people choose her life. As a leader, she can reproduce Tolliver's original mistake by deciding what a companion needs to know. Let someone she loves challenge that behavior, and make her change something concrete.

Maya's intended romantic dynamic with Tolliver requires real power as well as intelligence: give her earned abilities, decisive wins, and authority he respects. She can lead, disagree, and defeat meaningful opposition. Letting him take control is an active expression of her desire and trust. She remains the same formidable person when the encounter ends; neither tenderness nor surrender erases her competence.

**Sella:** A guard with ambitions, pleasure, pride, and an unfinished relationship to her father's instruction. Give her a professional decision that affects the rescue and later a journey she actively wants. She is not Maya's permanent tutorial, a reward for progress, or a disposable stage on the way to a man. Her coast desire receives an actual payoff by Book Three.

**Tolliver:** Skillful, attractive, accustomed to controlling what other people know. Better conduct is meaningful only when honesty costs him something. Give him an opportunity to earn privately by selling access or information; let his refusal be visible and costly. His relationships with Ordway, Hale, employers, and Sella exist independently of Maya. A kiss does not complete his accountability.

Tolliver's intended attraction centers on Maya's strength. He knows what she can do, wants her to become more capable, and is fascinated by being the person she chooses to let overwhelm her. His obsession is making her lose her composure, not diminishing her confidence, silencing her, or controlling her life. Establish his physical advantage through demonstrated skill and strength with credible limits; do not assume that he wins every contest or inflate his power whenever she advances. He can take the lead decisively when she wants it and release control when she withdraws. His earlier manipulation must be confronted and changed before this dynamic can read as earned trust.

**Mara and Iven:** The reunion belongs to them and Iven's sons as well as to the protagonist. Iven has skills, opinions, and years of family history. Rescue does not obligate him to become her expedition guide. Allow anger and awkwardness alongside the joy of reunion.

**Dain and Harra:** Expertise with limits. Their work creates opportunities Maya can act on. They can disagree without another full safety lecture. Dain must confront the consequence of the old closure through a present choice.

**Pell and Vey:** Pell's flooded fields are a real obligation, not a device for making her oppose Maya. Vey's ability to make coercive arrangements sound useful is a source of conflict; require an overt choice when compromise fails. Neither needs to become incompetent so the young protagonist can act.

Keep a core traveling cast small enough that each member has a job in the adventure and a life outside it. Return to home-base characters when consequences reach them. Do not add another permanent companion every time Maya wins a fight.

## Romance across an ongoing series

**Binding Maya–Tolliver direction:** “She is powerful and capable. He knows it. He can overpower her anyway. She chooses to let him. And his obsession is making her lose control, not making her small.” This is the intended arc, not an event already established in the manuscript.

Build the dynamic through action: Maya succeeds using her own strength; Tolliver's response makes his admiration and desire specific; an encounter establishes his greater physical leverage without manufacturing helplessness; she deliberately invites him to take control. Show his attention to her reactions and her desire to surrender her composure. Carry the effect into the aftermath, where he still respects her decisions and she retains her standing. A kiss, jealousy, rescue, or simple size difference alone does not deliver this promise. Keep the intimate decision, sexual payoff, and emotional consequences on page. Do not use fade-to-black to avoid a freely chosen encounter once it begins.

The two attractions are established; their final structure is not. Develop an honest, specific arrangement before a Book One climax depends on its durability. Keep the author's actual-age range of nineteen to twenty-three for Maya's current sexual partners and establish ages on page. Eiran and Vey remain excluded.

Sella's requested relationship must include mutual desire, a freely chosen intimate encounter at an appropriate point, and an aftermath that changes their behavior. Preserve the same requirements for Tolliver if his relationship reaches that stage. Neither encounter is wages, gratitude for rescue, instruction, or payment for staying.

The Book One endpoint should contain a positive commitment the participants understand, even if the wider relationship is still evolving. Nonexclusivity alone is not an emotional ending. What are they actually choosing to do together, what can each decline, and what happens when their work pulls them apart?

Later tension can come from incompatible jobs, trust under leadership, privacy within a group, unequal growth, and the cost of a shared home. Do not reset the first kiss, manufacture a breakup each volume, or require every new attractive person to join the relationship. No public package should imply a particular endgame before that creative decision is made.

## Checks before any volume is called complete

- Its advertised local objective is resolved on page. The climax depends on abilities and relationships developed earlier in that volume.
- Maya causes the decisive change. Companions retain indispensable expertise and choices.
- There are pleasure, humor, wonder, attraction, and earned victories as well as cost and fear.
- A gain changes the next action the reader sees. A loss changes a later choice.
- At least one major outstanding question gets a durable answer. The next hook concerns a new consequence or undertaking.
- The romance has advanced and the chosen commitments survive an actual test.
- Home means an increasingly specific place and set of people here. Earth does not become the next prize.
- Beta readers can identify the slow section, their favorite relationship, a remembered ability, and why they want the next book. Their responses are evidence to gather, not praise to assume.
