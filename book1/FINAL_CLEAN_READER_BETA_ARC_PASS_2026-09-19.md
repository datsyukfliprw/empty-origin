## 2. Priority repair chains

### R01 — Restore Pell's pronouns and Mara's kinship

**Status: confirmed corrections.**

Pell is established with she/her pronouns. Chapter 14 describes the flooded-seedling conversation using “she”; Chapter 15 also clearly uses female pronouns for Pell. The later text slips:

- Chapter 30: after Pell sees Maya and Sella and retreats into the counting room, “Bless him.” should be **“Bless her.”**
- Chapter 32: “Pell rubs one hand over his mouth” should use **her**.
- The Chapter 32 price negotiation continues with “He names another,” “He points out,” and the nod after Maya counts the money. Those references to Pell should use **she**.
- When Pell introduces the caravan offer, “I look at him. He looks too innocent” should refer to **her**.

Do not make a global replacement of male pronouns: Tolliver, Vey, and Iven remain separate speakers and subjects throughout these scenes.

Chapter 15 also calls the boys' letter “her sons' letter” when Mara begins reading it. Iven is her brother and the boys are his sons. The correction is **“her nephews' letter.”**

**Acceptance check:** Read Chapters 30 and 32 with every pronoun assigned to its actual antecedent. Then reread the identification/letter/reunion chain in Chapters 15, 17, and 29 without changing Mara's relationship to Iven.

### R02 — Clarify who is providing medical treatment

**Status: consequential continuity question; not proof that a Keeper cannot also have medical skills.**

Chapters 19–23 use a separately identified house mender for Maya's ankle examination and restrictions. Mara is established as the records keeper and Iven's sister. Beginning in Chapter 26, Mara performs the examinations, prescribes loading restrictions, applies the splint, and becomes the authority who clears powered work.

That medical role continues through Chapters 27–32, including Iven's immediate post-rescue treatment, Maya's recovery schedule, and the later clearance for Stepguard.

A reader needs to know whether this is an intentional second qualification or an accidental transfer of the mender's role. The manuscript does not give the transition enough support.

**Preferred repair with the least new canon:** Keep the house mender responsible for clinical judgments and clearance. Keep Mara present as sister, keeper, and someone enforcing the mender's instructions. Preserve her family reactions and all her records work.

**Alternative:** If Mara is deliberately medically qualified, establish the qualification and explain the handoff before she begins replacing the house mender. Do not solve it by silently assuming all Keepers are healers.

**Acceptance check:** Trace Chapters 19, 21–23, and 26–32. Every examination, treatment order, recovery restriction, and clearance must have a consistent source. Do not globally rename Mara.

### R03 — Reconcile Brace's apparent remote action with its established teaching

**Status: mechanics clarification required before release.**

The early teaching gives Brace a chosen line, sound support, bodily load, and a short duration. Chapter 23's advancement expressly does not introduce a new power.

Chapter 24 then says:

> “Pressure slams through my shoulders even though I am not touching the arm.”

It continues by saying the ability finds the chosen object and its support. In Chapter 25, Brace catches the trolley track “through the stone support above me.”

This reads as a substantial extension of targeting or range. The reader has not been given a clear acquisition, physical connection, or limit for that extension. A good tactical decision should not depend on a new capability becoming available in the sentence that needs it.

**Preferred repair:** Make the physical contact and load path explicit while preserving the existing cost, duration, and support limits. Do not add an unbounded remote-holding ability as a late proof fix.

**Alternative:** If remote anchoring is intended, establish the lesson or discovery before the decisive use, including its limits and why this application is valid.

The Chapter 29 rescue must be checked afterward. It already places Maya's hand on the near rail earlier in the sequence; do not automatically classify that use as remote. Verify the actual connection when the target becomes the near stone.

**Acceptance check:** The reader can answer what Maya touches or connects to, what bears the load, what she can hold, and why holding cannot stop the boundary itself. Preserve the crucial release decisions.

### R04 — Show the transition from written contact to audible speech

**Status: missing communication transition.**

Chapter 15 explicitly distinguishes written answers from hearing Iven:

> “We haven't heard him,” I say. “He answered the writing.”

The narration subsequently describes Mara speaking to a person who cannot hear. Mirrors, slates, and relays carry the contact work.

By Chapter 26, Harra speaks through the upper observation sleeve and “His voice arrives thin through the gap.” Extended direct conversation follows. Chapter 27 continues the audible exchanges.

It is plausible that changed access or a different opening permits sound. The problem is that this change arrives without being established, despite the previous importance of indirect communication.

**Repair:** Identify the first point where ordinary speech becomes audible and state what has changed. If the upper sleeve remains unsuitable for speech, keep those exchanges as written or relayed messages. Do not introduce a new magical communication power merely to simplify dialogue.

**Acceptance check:** The participants' locations and the channel of communication remain intelligible from the first contact through the rescue.

### R05 — Repair East Relief blocking and its stated retreat rules

**Status: action continuity repair plus rule clarification.**

**The trolley sequence, Chapter 25.** The trolley reaches the far side; Harra jumps off and locks it. Later the track is released and Harra rides the trolley to the far wall again. The reader is missing a necessary move or is being given the same arrival twice.

Choose one physical sequence. For example, an initial stop short of the landing would leave the later arrival to complete the crossing; an actual earlier arrival would require an explicit reason and movement that puts Harra and the trolley back out. Verify the brake controls and each person's position with the chosen repair.

**The floor rule, Chapters 23–24.** The expedition terms say that if a floor section shifts after being marked stable, the expedition ends. Chapter 24 tests and marks plates, then a plate shifts under Tolliver. The party rescues him and continues by the upper route.

Clarify whether that particular plate had been marked stable. If it had, the response must match the stated rule, or the text must honestly acknowledge a changed decision rather than present it as uncomplicated compliance. A more nuanced rerouting rule must be established before the incident, not invented retroactively to excuse it.

**The ankle rule, Chapters 23–25.** The actual departure rule is stop and reassess an increase in pain; turn back for sharp or persistent pain, instability, or swelling. Chapter 24 restates this as “increase in pain means turn back,” then correctly depicts a rest and return to baseline. Change the restatement to **“increase in pain means stop and check.”**

Chapter 25's safe-room accusation that Maya knew she should have turned back at the gallery also needs checking. It may be an emotional hindsight judgment, but should not rewrite the earlier recheck into a clear violation of a rule she actually followed.

**Acceptance check:** Read the entire entrance-to-exit action without relying on planning notes. Every position, route change, and invocation of a stop rule must agree with what the scene has shown.

### R06 — Reconcile the wage, contract, and settlement chain

**Status: financial continuity reconciliation; do not guess a replacement final total.**

**Chapter 21's purse.** The text says:

> “My purse has forty-six bits from before the extension. Today's eight makes fifty-four.”

The preceding sequence appears to omit one eight-bit wage. The six initial reading wages plus Maya's original coins produce 54 bits; the shield costs 24. The first two extra days bring her back to 46. Chapter 20 is explicitly the last paid day of that agreement, so another eight-bit day would bring her to 54 before the next extension, and Chapter 21's wage to 62.

That conclusion assumes the Chapter 20 wage has actually been paid. If it has not, show the outstanding amount. Do not invent an unseen purchase merely to preserve a number.

**Chapters 23 and 26.** Chapter 23 pays the one-day extension before departure, then establishes a new two-day recovery contract. Chapter 26 subsequently says “My Day Eighteen extension is settled under the existing rate” during the expedition settlement. This appears to name the already-settled contract instead of the two expedition days.

**Chapters 31–32.** The later reference to a Day Eighteen agreement and twelve days without updated paper no longer cleanly describes the contract sequence, because the recovery agreement exists on the page. The expanded interval after Sella's departure also needs to be reflected in any dated accounting.

The final multiplication is correct: **12 days × 8 bits = 96 bits = 12 pieces.** This review does not establish that the number of billable days must be changed. Calendar days, paid days, consulted days, medical retention, and released time must be separated first.

**Required working ledger:** For each relevant day, record the governing agreement, the wage earned, whether it has already been paid, any personal spending, and whether the final settlement includes it. Use only manuscript-supported entries or make the necessary clarification explicit.

**Related clarity query:** Chapter 32 requests a credit for the unused return cart, then settles a room price and pays 28 bits. State whether that price is net of the credit or itemize the credit; the current transaction does not visibly close that request.

**Acceptance check:** Chapters 17–23, 26, 31, and 32 agree. Nothing is paid twice, omitted without explanation, or retroactively invented.

### R07 — Place the post-departure roof scene within the recovery restrictions

**Status: temporal clarity repair.**

Chapter 30 describes six more days off the ankle, then later clearances for light shield work and stairs. Within the same recovery montage, “On the fourth evening” Maya takes bread to the pantry roof, previously reached by a ladder.

The natural reading is the fourth evening after Sella leaves, which is earlier than the stated clearance. The placement within the montage also makes the reference day unnecessarily uncertain.

**Repair:** Put an explicit date relative to the clearance, move the scene to an accessible location, or establish safe assistance appropriate to the restrictions. Preserve the emotional purpose of the roof memory and the later experience of someone occupying Sella's seat.

**Acceptance check:** Keep the nine-night separation before Chapter 31. Check that the medical permissions, location access, and timing agree without compressing Sella's absence.

## 5. Beta-reader assessment

### Opening and desire to continue

The opening establishes Maya's competence before exposing her vulnerability. Her gaming attention remains relevant to coordination and decision-making rather than functioning only as a decorative backstory. Tolliver's assistance feels useful and unsettling at the same time. That tension gives the early material more interest than an uncomplicated rescue-romance setup.

The market fire supplies a concrete payoff for Maya's way of reading a group under pressure. The Earth identification card then creates a compelling new question. Preserve those functions.

### Middle: the principal attention-risk zone

My strongest attention-risk areas are **Chapters 20–22 and 26–28**. Repeated measurements, test conditions, equipment modifications, and re-statements of the release principle accumulate faster than some readers will want to process them.

This is not a recommendation to remove the engineering plot. The individual failures generally change the problem, and the details often carry real stakes. The useful distinction is between a genuinely new constraint and another explanation of a constraint the reader already understands.

Any compression should target the latter. Preserve the failure evidence, the changed decision, the cost, and the subsequent payoff. Do not give every test the same full sequence of setup, safety list, result, interpretation, and thematic summary.

The recurring fragment patterns and “No / Good / Of course” exchanges also become more noticeable in the late procedural chapters. Treat adjacent duplicates and interchangeable character reactions selectively. A blanket removal would flatten Maya's established voice.

### Progression and expedition appeal

East Relief provides a genuine bounded expedition: a reason to enter, environmental rules, finite reserve, indispensable companions, a necessary recovery, injury cost, and an exit that matters. The blocking and rules issues in R05 prevent it from being fully clean, but the excursion belongs in the book.

The progression emphasizes judgment, equipment, technique, and team function. Maya finishes at level six with two active abilities and recognized release technique rather than an extensive new power menu. That is a specific reading experience, not a defect by definition.

Packaging should fit that experience: rescue-and-expedition LitRPG with a strong romantic engine, not an immediate dungeon-loot grind or an exclusive-couple romance promise. The first substantial recovery delve arrives late; do not sell a different opening from the one this book contains.

### Romance and emotional continuity

Sella is not merely preparation for Tolliver. She has professional aims, a destination, a cost for delaying her work, and limits on waiting. Her departure is emotionally effective because love does not cancel those aims.

The nine-day interval before Tolliver's major scene helps the transition. Preserve the absence, the roof/seat memories after correcting their chronology, and the explicit refusal to treat Sella as gone from Maya's emotional life.

Tolliver's development works best where behavior changes: he supplies information that does not flatter him, refuses compromised work, respects decisions, and follows a release call under pressure. The intimate payoff recalls the original control problem instead of pretending it never happened. Chosen surrender is not presented as retroactive permission for his earlier conduct.

The closing relationship is deliberately nonexclusive and still being negotiated. Do not silently turn it into either a settled exclusive couple or an already-agreed three-person relationship.

### Climax and ending

Iven's reunion is the clearest emotional payoff. The frame is expendable; the person is not. The rescue succeeds through coordinated judgment rather than a last-minute unlimited power. Repairing the mechanics should strengthen that existing result, not replace it.

Rusk remains competent and self-interested rather than becoming foolish to permit victory. The immediate custody/access pressure is contained without pretending the entire legal dispute has vanished.

**Keep the current ending.** The paid room, key, chest, shield, retained Earth memories, Sella's continuing importance, Tolliver's chosen relationship, and the new escort job make belonging concrete. Maya is choosing a future rather than receiving a speech announcing that she has healed.

“Home, apparently, has chores” is an appropriate final turn for this book. Do not inflate it into prophecy, a larger villain reveal, or a sudden return-home tease.

### Deliberately open questions

Elena's whereabouts, the network's larger history, the full explanation of Maya's arrival, the remaining salvage hearing, and the relationships' future can remain open as series material. Their openness is not automatically a Book One failure.

The book still owes readers clarity about the rules used to resolve its immediate rescue. That is why R03–R06 matter more at this stage than adding another lore explanation.
