# Rescue mechanics — writer's working model

This is design material, not an in-world document. The chapters govern canon. Unrevealed explanations remain provisional and must be established through action before a rescue relies on them.

The sleeve looks diagonally down into the chamber. Its instrument can reach the near sill with a slender measuring extension; a person cannot fit through it. A probe can remain wholly on Rook's side. The lower service route bends past the wheel recess and is the future human access, still closed through Chapter Nineteen. The upper spindle clamp restrains the displaced setting; it does not repair the missing retaining tooth or control every part of the transit junction.

Sixteen establishes a roughly six-local-foot gap between the far-side lower stub and Iven's remaining three steps, with some uncertainty in endpoint choice. Iven uses the sound section of his own old local rule; mirrored views establish which divisions to count, not an optical scale comparison. No through-line joins the two spaces yet. Iven has a removable bed-frame crosspiece long enough to reach across the gap, but too narrow/weak for a person; it can eventually carry a light messenger line. Human retrieval needs independently supported bridging, equipment and boundary timing still unavailable.

## Ordinary water

The failed bypass loads the old junction. A candidate separate route uses the northern field collector and an abandoned surface wash channel to the downstream mill tailrace. Tolliver's bank survey and fresh levels must establish its feasibility. This route does not use the warm/gritty outlet. A trial notch can demonstrate fall and expose obstruction without draining the whole settlement. Final opening requires shoring the abandoned channel's failed masonry throat; the crew's long lifting equipment has immediate practical value.

## Unrevealed boundary model

Default from the series bible: the restrained wheel fixes local coupling, while a damaged receiving selector responds to loading independently. A pressure rise reaches a threshold, engages a limited interval, then a falling threshold ends it with hysteresis. The displayed width is a moving boundary; its edge can sever a crossing line. Mere time of day is not the control. Before the climax, compare upstream condition, outlet pulses, width and repeatability at both ends; do not infer a usable human interval from one test. No exact period or safe duration is canon yet. No future surprise capacity to freeze the boundary with Warder magic.

The receiving rooms are in this world, with imported Earth fittings. Establish local structure, matched plan, exterior and finite power/food/water sources in later chapters. Do not declare the destination from English or one unfamiliar view.

## Progression decisions for this continuation

- Sixteen: level 2 at 13/14 → level 3 at 0/18. Maximum reserve 4 → 5; current reserve unchanged by advancement. Two Braces spent during measured rig work, 4 → 2, leaving 2/5. No healing or automatic ability.
- Seventeen: after sleep and food 4/5; no spend. No observed progress change.
- Eighteen: morning 5/5. Sella teaches **Stepguard**, costing 2 reserve per attempt once committed, including a broken attempt. Maximum two breaths, one short straight step forward or backward in the direction already chosen; at least one foot retains sound support. No pivot, leap, sliding ground or free additional step. The pattern protects the shield's facing against a bounded load; it neither encloses the user nor stops transit. Cannot layer with Brace; release one before the other. Two attempts, first fails on pivot, second succeeds under a light straight push: 5 → 3 → 1/5; recorded progress 3/18. Further mastery remains ahead.
- Nineteen: morning 3/5 after food/rest, no automatic full recovery. One Brace (3 → 2), one Stepguard (2 → 0), no refill during action. Stepguard succeeds along its chosen line, then ends when an intended landing fails under load. Recorded progress 9/18 after operation; no level 4. Left ankle injury and damaged shield persist.

Recovery is observed, not a universal two-points-per-night rule. Progress is recorded at meaningful endpoints without pretending to know itemized awards.
