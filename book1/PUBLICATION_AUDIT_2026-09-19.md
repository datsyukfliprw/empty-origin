## 1. Continuity audit

### Repaired: East Relief ankle abort rule

Chapter 23 originally established two absolute rules:

- Maya turns back at the first increase in pain, instability or swelling.
- If the ankle worsens, she turns back.

Chapter 24 then explicitly says the pain increases on the upper stair, but Maya rests, checks it and continues. Both scenes were individually sensible, but the wording made continuing a violation of the plan.

The repair keeps the intended scene and consequence:

- any increase in pain triggers a stop and recheck
- sharp pain, pain that does not settle, instability, or swelling triggers retreat
- Chapter 24 now states that the extra ache settles back to its entrance level before Maya continues

This preserves the later cost. Maya still pushes too far during the delve, worsens the ankle, and pays for it in the following chapters.

### Current continuity chain that holds

- The early local-day chronology remains the repaired sequence already recorded in `CONTINUITY_REVIEW.md`.
- Maya's ankle injury begins in the drainage operation, worsens in East Relief, receives a three-day no-loading restriction, receives limited rescue-day clearance, worsens again during extraction, and is then rehabilitated over the post-rescue time jump.
- Sella's eastbound work remains her own choice. She accepts before the rescue, stays through the rescue window, leaves after Iven is home, and is not converted into an obstacle to Tolliver.
- Rusk's salvage/preservation claim stays separate from human custody. Maya gives up contested iron rather than gambling Iven for answers.
- Iven remains trapped until Chapter 29. The failed Chapter 28 human attempt does not quietly become a partial rescue.
- The ending does not reopen Earth as Maya's destination. The second receiving-house lead is a local-world expedition objective.

### Live mysteries that are not continuity defects

- Elena's present location
- Maya's blank origin
- the larger receiving-station network
- the eventual disposition of disputed far-side iron
- Sella's later relationship status and return

These should stay unresolved unless a later-book plan deliberately answers them.

## 2. System mechanics audit

### Repaired: Stepguard definition versus Chapter 24 use

The original teaching scene defined Stepguard through the shield strongly enough that Chapter 24's shieldless use read like a new rule invented under pressure.

The repair now establishes during Chapter 18 training that:

- Stepguard is fundamentally a stance/support-transfer pattern
- a shield is the normal and safest load surface, but is not required for the pattern to exist
- the user must keep a chosen line/facing
- at least one foot must retain sound support
- the movement is one short straight step
- turning, jumping, or reaching for an extra step breaks the pattern
- it does not enclose the user or stop transit

`RESCUE_MECHANICS.md` was updated to match. Chapter 24's shieldless use is now an application of a taught rule rather than an expansion discovered in the instant of need.

### Progression chain

The late-book progression is mechanically coherent:

- Level 3: maximum reserve 5
- Level 4: maximum reserve 6
- Level 5: maximum reserve 7
- Level 6: maximum reserve 8

Advancement increases capacity without refilling current reserve. The manuscript explicitly preserves that rule at each major late advancement.

The book also correctly avoids level-up healing. Maya's ankle remains injured across Levels 4, 5 and 6.

### Ability versus technique distinction

This remains clean:

- **Brace** is an activated Warder ability.
- **Stepguard** is an activated Warder ability with a two-reserve commitment.
- **Release Under Load** begins as a System-recognized technique, not a button.
- **Controlled Release** is a refinement/recognition update, not a surprise new activated power.

That distinction is one of the strongest LitRPG choices in the book because the System names learned judgment instead of replacing judgment.

### Final rescue

The rescue pays off established mechanics rather than introducing a climax-only solution:

- independent support
- bounded movement
- short far pin
- spring travel
- release-before-unload
- floating/captured near head
- sacrificial structure
- pressure and vibration observation
- Iven's own far-side participation
- Brace used on the near stone for a specific purpose rather than as universal reinforcement
- deliberate release when continued holding would transfer destructive load

No universal safe interval, portal-freeze power, automatic heal, or unexplained reserve refill is required.

## 3. Romance audit

### Maya and Sella

The relationship functions as a real romance, not a temporary female fling before the male endgame.

What works:

- Sella has independent work, money, travel goals and professional competence.
- The physical relationship has its own texture and emotional aftermath.
- Maya explicitly loves her.
- Sella's departure costs both of them something.
- Sella does not demand that Maya erase Tolliver.
- Their conflict is about the kind of future Sella can live inside, not about Maya's sexuality.
- The ending keeps Sella possible without freezing her life off page.

Protect this.

### Maya and Tolliver

The late arc successfully converts the relationship from attraction plus control problems into chosen trust without retroactively romanticizing the early control.

The crucial earned steps are behavioral:

- he discloses information before Maya has to extract it
- he refuses ethically compromised tracking work
- he follows Maya's correct calls under pressure
- he stops when told
- he lets professional role assignments override impulse
- he asks before carrying/touching when the distinction matters
- he gives Sella and Maya room
- he does not use Sella's departure as an opening
- Maya initiates Chapter 31 after recovery time

Chapter 31 delivers the binding erotic promise: Maya is capable, he knows she is capable, he can overpower her physically, she chooses to yield, and his erotic fixation is her loss of composure rather than her diminishment.

### Remaining romance line-edit issue

The scenes often demonstrate agency clearly and then Maya narrates the meaning again. The meaning is important, but repeated explanation reduces heat because the reader is being handed the interpretation after already feeling it.

The final romance polish should preserve explicit consent checks at actual escalation points while trimming redundant commentary such as repeated variants of:

- he did not decide for me
- he stopped immediately
- no ownership / no contest
- that matters
- good
- not jealousy / not accusation

The goal is not fewer boundaries. It is fewer explanations after a boundary has already been dramatized.

## 4. Pacing and chapter-function audit

### Structural verdict

The final architecture works and should not be rebuilt.

The sequence has a clear escalation:

- investigation and bounded tests
- failed span
- resource problem
- East Relief recovery delve
- mechanical lesson and gear recovery
- prototype and rehabilitation
- legal pressure
- failed human extraction
- changed design
- successful extraction
- Sella reckoning/departure
- Maya/Tolliver payoff
- economic/property settlement
- next-expedition choice

Every late chapter changes at least one of: build, relationship, rescue state, legal position, injury state, or future direction.

### Highest-value compression targets

**Procedural middle:** Keep measurements that change a decision. Compress repeated recitations of conditions that have not changed.

**Chapter 27:** The rehab, full-frame build, dry testing, Rusk notice and rescue clearance all belong here, but repeated specifications can be shortened because the reader already understands the spring-foot logic from Chapters 25 and 26.

**Chapter 32:** The economic/property payoff is thematically important and should stay. It can tolerate a light trim where contract clauses are repeated after the reader already understands Maya now knows how to negotiate.

### Chapters that should keep their breathing room

- Chapter 29 reunion
- Chapter 30 Sella departure
- Chapter 31 first fully realized Maya/Tolliver encounter
- final choice in Chapter 32

Compressing those for raw speed would damage payoff.

## 5. Full voice and repetition pass

A whole-draft phrase scan confirms that the repetition problem is concentrated in the final third rather than evenly distributed.

Current whole-draft counts after this pass include:

- `Of course`: 31
- `I know.`: 66
- `Good.`: 55
- `I hate...`: 52
- `Not because`: 30
- `That matters`: 6

Counts alone are not defects. Maya is allowed verbal habits. The problem is when several habits stack around the same kind of beat.

### Main rhetorical echo

The final third repeatedly uses:

> Not X.  
> Not Y.  
> The real interpretation.  
> That matters / Good / I hate how much I notice.

That cadence is effective when reserved for a major realization. Repeated every few pages, it becomes visible machinery.

### Edits already applied

Surgical repetition trims were made in Chapters 23, 24, 25 and 27. These cuts remove explanatory restatement while preserving plot, reserve math, consent, relationship state and characterization.

The pass deliberately did **not** globally replace Maya's common words. Her humor and fragments are part of the voice. A final line pass should judge each occurrence by function.

### What to protect

- gamer analogies when they reveal how Maya thinks, not just because a joke is available
- retail/work/economic attention
- bodily irritation and practical problem-solving
- humor under stress
- family-specific Earth memories
- short fragments at genuinely high-pressure moments
- her increasing contractual/legal fluency as character progression

## 6. Dialogue fingerprint pass

### Maya

Distinct. Fast defensive humor, questions that become negotiations, gaming vocabulary, practical money awareness, emotional truth arriving late and often too directly once it breaks through.

### Tolliver

Distinct. Short answers, low verbal ornament, careful qualification, strong use of waiting/silence. His late-book dialogue works best when the narration does not immediately explain every restraint.

### Sella

Distinct. Teasing plus blunt emotional diagnosis. She speaks from work/road reality and is more willing than Maya to name the relationship problem directly.

Watch item: Maya and Sella can both default to clipped joke/counter-joke exchanges. Preserve moments where Sella's answers are plainer and less performative than Maya's.

### Harra

Distinct. Structure first. Concrete nouns, tolerances, failure modes, buildability. Her mechanical joy is characterizing.

### Mara

Distinct. Medical authority, terse imperatives, refusal to bargain with bodies. She can overlap Harra in bluntness, but their domains keep them legible.

### Rusk

Distinct. Procedural leverage, legal exactness, professional threat without melodrama. Do not make her snarkier; her lack of Maya-style humor is part of her pressure.

### Vey

Distinct enough. Commercial/legal intelligence with a smoother social surface than Rusk. Best when he talks around price/rights rather than sounding like another engineer.

### Dain and Oret

Primary watch item.

Both often speak in short technical corrections about pressure, structure and measurement. Their functions are different enough to sharpen further:

- **Dain:** diagnostic caution, stone/contact evidence, refuses conclusions beyond the measurement.
- **Oret:** fabrication/load path, asks what can be built, moved, replaced or tested.

A dialogue-only polish can reinforce those tendencies without adding verbal gimmicks.

### Iven

Generally distinct through dry survival humor and fatherhood. Watch for occasional Maya-like joke cadence. His humor should feel older, drier and less eager to turn every stress beat into a bit.
