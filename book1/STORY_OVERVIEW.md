# Story overview

This cumulative synopsis records the revised manuscript through Chapter Nineteen. The September 19 cold-review revision changed the prose throughout and materially changed Chapters Twelve–Nineteen. Written chapter text governs canon; older editorial reviews describe earlier versions. Character reports, deductions and untested mechanisms remain distinguished from observed facts. Maya never returns to Earth under the author's series direction; her early desire to do so remains part of the story.

## Chapter 01

Maya Szczepaniak, a nineteen-year-old community-college student and coffee-shop worker in Kansas City, directed her online raid through its final dangerous movement. Playing the elf spellblade Nyx, she stopped attacking long enough to wait for a safe gap in the boss's fire, kept Liam from following her into danger, and called the group's movement before rejoining the attack. Together they defeated Vharos the Undying in six minutes and forty-two seconds. Eleven viewers watched her stream. The achievement offered both camaraderie and a glimpse of the streaming career she hoped would eventually replace her closing shifts at Brewed Awakening.

Away from the game, Maya's independence was precarious but important to her. Her small apartment had unreliable fixtures and very little food, while her parents had made clear that she could return home. She remembered their practical affection and her father's humor without wanting to surrender the life she was trying to build. A recollection of teaching a classroom to pronounce Szczepaniak established her changing relationship with her own identity: the name she once disliked had become something she valued. She cooked eggs, returned to the ongoing call, and won Vharos's Emberglass Saber with a roll of ninety-eight. She immediately equipped it and began considering cosmetic changes to Nyx's armor.

A sudden physical crisis interrupted the celebration. Dizziness, ringing in her ears, and difficulty moving worsened until she could not reach the phone on her desk. Her friend Liam stopped calling her Nyx and used her real name. Other people on the call became alarmed and discussed getting help, but Maya could not provide useful information or contact her parents. Her last awareness of Earth was of her desk, the headset, the food, and the victorious character still on her screen. The cause of the crisis and what happened to her body or apartment afterward were not established.

Maya woke in a rain-soaked clearing beneath an unfamiliar sky. She remained in her own human body, wearing sleep shorts and an oversized CRIT HAPPENS shirt, with no shoes, phone, or equipment. Enormous pale trees, blue-black leaves, and distant mountains contradicted every ordinary explanation she tried. Panic escalated as she thought of her parents and called for her father without receiving an answer. Approaching lightning finally gave her an immediate problem she could act on. Unable to explain her situation, she forced herself to leave the exposed clearing and enter the forest.

## Chapter 02

The forest gave Maya partial shelter from the storm but made movement dangerous. She tried to navigate downhill in hopes of finding water, roads, and eventually people, relying on improvised reasoning rather than wilderness experience. Mist obscured her landmarks, the ground hid sharp objects, and her bare feet sank into wet vegetation. When something cut the arch of her left foot, she tore a strip from the hem of her shirt and used it as a bandage. Continuing to walk aggravated the injury, but remaining where she was offered no clear protection.

As darkness fell, cold and exhaustion impaired her ability to act. Maya found a hollow beneath a fallen tree's root mass, checked briefly for an occupant, and crawled inside. She covered herself with leaves for insulation. In that shelter, thoughts of her mother and father became harder to suppress. She imagined their reactions to her disappearance, although she had no knowledge of what they were actually doing on Earth.

A large creature passed close to the hollow during the night. From her limited viewpoint, Maya saw a pale, oddly jointed leg and a broad, four-toed foot. A faint green glow moved above the visible portion of its body. She remained hidden until it left. The creature's identity, intelligence, and level were not established; the encounter demonstrated how little protection her improvised shelter provided.

At dawn she emerged injured, hungry, and exhausted. A rabbitlike animal with forward-facing eyes carried floating text. The unfamiliar marks shifted into comprehensible language, identifying a briar-skipper at level three. Maya recognized the display as something resembling a game nameplate. She saw another distant plate but could find none above herself. Spoken commands such as status and inventory did not open an interface.

Because her attempts failed, Maya concluded that the creatures around her belonged to a System that she herself was outside. This was her interpretation of incomplete evidence, not an established fact about the world. She contrasted her vulnerable body with Nyx's familiar health bars and equipment, reminding herself that her avatar still belonged to a game on Earth. Before she could decide what to do next, footsteps approached. A human figure came uphill through the mist beneath the plate TRAVELER, level eleven. Maya stayed in the open because the prospect of contact outweighed her fear of the unknown arrival.

## Chapter 03

The traveler stopped at a careful distance, presenting himself as unhurried and nonthreatening. Maya treated that restraint as evidence that he might help her, while recognizing how desperately she wanted a reason to trust someone. She also noticed his youth and attractiveness before fully assessing his weapons and equipment. He carried a bow and a shorter weapon, wore a well-fitting coat, and appeared far more comfortable in the forest than she was.

His speech initially reached Maya as unfamiliar sounds before resolving into understandable words. The translation resembled the way she had learned to read the briar-skipper's plate. She noticed the delay but could not investigate it while cold, injured, and relieved to hear another person. The traveler looked above her head before studying her face and clothing. He asked whether she was alone, what she ran, and eventually who had set her plate. Maya could understand the words but lacked the cultural and mechanical context needed to answer them.

When she tried saying status aloud, he reacted with slight amusement rather than an explanation. Her bleeding foot became the immediate practical concern. He examined it with her permission, and she held his shoulder for balance. His warmth made her understand how cold she had become. He then told her to close the wound, apparently expecting an ability she did not know how to use. Her inability to comply changed his assessment of her, though his precise motives remained hidden.

The traveler gave Maya water and replaced her dirty makeshift bandage with clean cloth. He warned that continuing to walk on the untreated injury would soon create a worse problem and offered to take her to a settlement. She accepted because the alternative was another night alone in the forest. He discouraged her from admitting her ignorance to anyone else and suggested she let her appearance make people willing to help.

As they traveled downhill, he supported her injured side, provided information about the route, and told a funny story. His practical care and physical closeness deepened her relief and dependence. Neither exchanged a name during this first encounter. He learned much about her vulnerability without asking where she came from, what had happened to her clothing, or whom she had lost. By the time Maya noticed that imbalance, she was wearing his coat and felt overwhelmingly grateful for his help.

## Chapter 04

Continuing the same morning, the traveler managed Maya's immediate needs, feeding her dried meat, offering water, and setting a pace that her injured foot could tolerate. As they followed a creek toward a cart track, Maya observed that some small creatures did not have visible plates. She also noticed him repeatedly touching the hollow at the base of his throat, although she did not yet understand the gesture. When she finally asked his name, he gave Tolliver, shortened to Toll, and she introduced herself as Maya.

Tolliver taught her practical rules about roads, bargaining, questions from strangers, and touching shoulders. When he told her to keep her plate shut, she insisted on understanding what he meant. He explained that the floating word above him was a chosen public label concealing more personal information. According to him, people selected ordinary labels that would attract little attention. Maya's own plate, which she could not see above herself, had been displaying her entire name. He admitted reading it from the bottom of the hill before their first conversation.

This revelation changed Maya's understanding of the apparent stranger who had helped her. He had approached already knowing something she did not know she was revealing. Nevertheless, his care and her lack of alternatives made her defer the implications. Following his instructions, she pressed two fingers to her throat and opened an internal display. It showed Maya Szczepaniak, level one, with an empty origin field. The correct appearance of her full Earth name deeply affected her. Her earlier conclusion that she was outside the System had been wrong.

Tolliver described origin as an accountable affiliation with a holding, house, company, or temple. He warned that an adult at level one would attract curiosity, while an empty origin could prevent entry through a gate. At her request he pronounced her surname correctly, apparently without needing to reread it. That accuracy offered comfort while also reinforcing how much he had learned before introducing himself.

He guided her to a list of public labels and recommended Household, describing it as indicating someone answerable for her. Maya selected it without understanding its wider consequences. Her level remained publicly visible. Tolliver then instructed her to let him answer all questions at the gate, even when addressed directly. He said the appearance that she belonged to him would protect her. Maya accepted the plan out of relief and necessity, and he supplied the name of a mill they passed as a truthful place of recent origin she could repeat.

## Chapter 05

That afternoon, traffic on the road showed Maya how normal concealed identities were. Travelers, workers, and tradespeople used ordinary public labels, and everyone read everyone else's plate. Approaching Greyward, she was emotionally overwhelmed by familiar smells of bread and work despite the unfamiliar setting. The settlement was much larger than Tolliver's word village had led her to expect: timber walls, gate towers, crowded buildings, and organized entry procedures made it a substantial town.

At the gate, people temporarily exposed their names and affiliations for inspection before concealing them again. Tolliver told Maya that her empty origin would be seen and that he would handle the explanation. When her turn came, the warden Bertran questioned her adult age, level one, lack of footwear, and missing affiliation. Tolliver supplied a detailed story about a fever destroying Skell's Holding and killing its clerk. Maya recognized the story as a prepared explanation but did not interrupt it. The actual existence and history of Skell's were not independently verified.

Eiran Hale, a man apparently in his thirties with an openly displayed name and level twenty-six, intervened. He studied Maya's plate longer than the other observers and insisted she answer his question about her age herself. She told him she was nineteen. His remark that someone must have kept her very safe suggested an interpretation of her low level but did not establish its cause. Hale told Bertran to admit her. As Maya and Tolliver entered, Hale asked whether she was Tolliver's, and Tolliver affirmed that she was.

Inside Greyward, Tolliver paid for a mender to treat Maya's foot. The mender's work was painful but closed the wound, leaving a hot pink seam and easing the distress in her leg. Tolliver also bought secondhand boots, dry clothing, and food. Each purchase increased Maya's awareness that she possessed no money or independent means of survival. His generosity was real, but she understood that it also concentrated her options in his hands.

He rented a room above a tavern, told her to sleep, and left on unspecified business. Alone with a bed, roof, and door, Maya finally grieved openly for her parents and former life. She folded her filthy Earth shirt and hid it beneath the straw mattress, preserving it as a link to home. Bolting the door gave her a first tangible boundary against the world. When Tolliver returned and identified himself, however, she opened it immediately and let him in.

## Chapter 06

On her third local calendar day, after one forest night and one night in Greyward, Maya woke to find the bolt drawn back and Tolliver seated beside the door. He said he had gone out and returned while she slept, then announced that he would be away for the day. He left four small coins for food and instructed her to be back by dark without explaining his destination. After he left, Maya recognized how automatically she had begun waiting for him to provide every next step. She decided to earn enough money for one purchase of her own and left his coins untouched.

Watching Greyward work, she concluded that Hand was a broad public label for laborers. She found a crew unloading barley and persuaded the foreman to let her attempt the work. Her apparent success in getting hired collapsed almost immediately: she could not carry a sack up the plank. A smaller, older woman at level fourteen lifted it away with ease. The comparison made Maya's low level feel like a practical limitation rather than merely a conspicuous number. She could not yet tell how experience, reserve, a run, or level itself produced the difference.

She shifted to a form of work she knew. At a busy dry-goods stall, Maya offered to handle customers while the seller dealt with a delivery. Ordway gave her prices, warned her away from the till, and promised a piece at the midday close. Maya inferred the relationship between the coins from transactions: eight thin bits equaled one larger piece. Her coffee-shop experience transferred well. She managed queues and changing orders, helped a customer with only one free hand, rejected a shaved coin, and challenged a salt delivery's claimed quantity. The boy she helped returned and asked specifically for her. Ordway offered work again the next morning.

At payment, however, Ordway noticed Maya's Household label and asked who received her wages. She explained that paying a household member directly could expose her to a claim from that person's house. She had previously faced a man carrying a stick at her stall and would not casually risk a repeat. Maya discovered that the label Tolliver had presented as protection also constrained her apparent right to be paid. Changing it in front of Ordway felt dangerous while the gate record still reflected Tolliver's account.

When Maya named Tolliver, Ordway reconsidered and placed the piece in her hand. Maya did not know whether fear, familiarity, sympathy, or some other history explained the change. She deliberately avoided treating one reaction as proof. She left with one earned piece, renewed work prospects, and a sharper awareness that even her first independent wage had reached her through his name.

## Chapter 07

Immediately after leaving Ordway, Maya spent one bit on bread and received seven in change. At the tavern she learned that the room cost four bits per night, that Tolliver had paid through the current night, and that he had told the keeper they would leave the following morning. Maya spent another bit on a half candle, hid her remaining six beneath her Earth shirt, and left Tolliver's four coins untouched.

Exploring her interface independently, she found a measure beneath her level: progress seven out of ten, reserve three out of three, and run unset. She could not determine which experiences had earned the progress or whether she had arrived with some of it. The reserve display could open into three marks, while the unset run did not offer a selection. She scratched reminders into the candle rather than assuming she understood the mechanics.

Tolliver returned with food, and Maya confronted him about her intended departure and the unpaid-wage problem. He admitted collecting a house's debt from Ordway the previous autumn after her husband died, taking half her stall. His account explained a connection but could not establish Ordway's motives for paying Maya. He denied having seen Ordway that day, but defended withholding information as necessary to get Maya through the gate. Maya challenged that defense. According to him, the chosen label had not magically bonded Maya to him, but the gate entry and other people's expectations still mattered.

Pressed for specifics, Tolliver described Rook House as a company holding offering shelter, training, and affiliation in exchange for work. He had told a factor about Maya's first name, adult age, low level, and lack of origin. He would receive a finding fee of six pieces if she entered. He knew the usual term was a year with instruction charged against an account, but had not brought the full written agreement or explained when he intended to disclose the fee. Maya refused to leave the next morning, insisted on returning to Ordway, and demanded the written terms before deciding anything. Tolliver agreed to delay a day.

At Maya's request, he taught her to use reserve by holding a water basin. Focusing on the unfamiliar internal resource produced a brief burst of strength and spent one point, leaving two out of three. He said food, rest, and time would replenish it, and that a run could be learned without joining a house, although instruction and practical understanding were needed. During the conversation he explicitly gave his age as twenty-two. Maya contrasted his three-year age advantage with sixteen years of experience using reserve.

The successful lesson briefly brought their attraction into the foreground. Maya accepted a gentle touch to her jaw but turned away before a kiss and said she would not pursue intimacy while he decided where she slept. He withdrew immediately. After he left, she spent four of her own bits to secure the same room for the following night. She ended with two personal bits, four untouched coins from Tolliver, reserve at two out of three, and a concrete alternative to leaving with him.

## Chapter 08

The following morning, Maya confirmed that her reserve had recovered to three out of three. Her level remained one, her progress seven out of ten, and her run unset. Tolliver had not returned during the night. She spent one personal bit on breakfast and went to Ordway's stall with one left. Ordway explained that naming Tolliver had shown her she was not dealing with an ordinary house's claim to a member's wages. Paying Maya had still been a choice involving risk, not a simple product of either kindness or intimidation. Their relationship became more candid as Maya asked directly for information.

Ordway also warned her against choosing a run merely to suit the stall. She said she had chosen a runner's path at thirteen, later regretted aspects of it, and described changing runs as possible but costly in work. An early repeat of ordinary selling did not change Maya's visible progress. Maya recognized that the choice of a run would affect her actual body and future rather than an easily reset avatar.

A delivery cart caught an awning rope, breaking an oil jar and spreading fire from a nearby charcoal pan. Hester, the cook, fell with her skirt trapped beneath a wheel and a collapsed table restricting her escape. Higher-level adults responded competently but could not free the interconnected cart, rope, pole, and cloth by simply lifting harder. Maya identified the rope constraint and directed the carter to cut it. While others lifted the cart, held the pole, and smothered the flames with sand, she spent two reserve points in succession to raise the table long enough for Ordway to pull Hester clear. The rescue depended on coordinated effort, not Maya overpowering everyone else.

Afterward Maya discovered that she had advanced to level two. Her new display read progress one out of fourteen, reserve one out of four, and run unset. The increased reserve capacity had not replenished what she had spent. She had singed hair, a shallow forearm scrape, red knuckles, sore feet, and oil-soaked boots. A mender briefly treated her hands; Ordway asked that it be charged to a fire account whose funding Maya did not yet understand. Reaching the milestone intensified her longing to tell Liam about it.

When Bertran arrived, Maya asked to correct the gate record rather than waiting for another person to decide her status. He offered an appointment after the midday bell with her keeper or employer as a witness. He did not promise an origin, permanent residence, or a completed change. Ordway agreed to accompany her after closing. Tolliver later found Maya at the stall, showed concern about her injuries, recognized her new level, and offered to attend if the gatehouse needed him.

Master Vey, Rook House's level-eighteen factor, arrived with a folded service offer. Maya did not read or accept it. He then produced a repaired plastic identification card bearing a woman's photograph and the English word MISSOURI. Unlike the local script, its letters required no translation for Maya to understand. She picked up the card and asked where the woman was. The chapter ended before Vey answered. The object established a tangible Earth connection, but the pictured woman's identity, survival, whereabouts, and means of arrival remained unknown.

## Chapter 09

Vey answered Maya's question by saying that he had last seen the woman alive, leaving Rook House angrily on foot four years earlier. Maya read the card herself: Elena Ruth Mercer, an Independence address, birth year 1993, issue year 2023, and expiration year 2029. The dates raised questions about elapsed time between worlds without resolving them. Vey said Elena had spent approximately nine months at Rook doing survey work with its keeper of records and had disputed the closure of the south sluice before leaving. He attributed possession of the card to a chest Elena had left behind; neither that account nor her present whereabouts was independently verified.

Tolliver said he had not seen the card before. He admitted that, that morning, he had told Vey about the writing on Maya's shirt and her initial inability to use reserve. He denied searching the forest for her, saying he had encountered her while returning from a message run. Maya challenged both men's practice of exchanging information about her without involving her. Unable to keep the card, she copied Elena's name, dates, and city onto a scrap supplied by Ordway. Maya could comprehend the offer's local script, while Vey, Ordway, and Tolliver indicated that they could not read her English copy.

The actual service offer required a year, paid two pieces weekly plus board, and allowed charges for equipment, clothing, and additional instruction. The holding would select her basic training to suit its needs. Departure depended on notice and settlement of charges; work and recovered objects belonged to the house. Maya refused and proposed a short reading hire instead. Vey identified a thirty-six-page notebook and associated labels as the immediate work. The midday appointment interrupted negotiation. Ordway paid the full second morning's wage, bringing Maya's personal money to nine bits.

At the gatehouse, Tolliver withdrew responsibility and disclaimed any wage claim or debt for his spending. Maya then disclosed that she had never been to Skell's. Questioned separately, she named Kansas City, Missouri, and the United States and described waking in the forest. Bertran recorded this as unverified, granting seven days' provisional stay on her own responsibility with employment attested by Ordway. Tolliver admitted inventing her connection to Skell's, paid a two-piece fine, and lost entry-witness standing pending Hale's review. Maya received a stamped slip and changed her public label to Traveler; her origin remained blank. Bertran also confirmed that the market fire fund covered her treatment. Tolliver apologized for concealing the finding fee and sharing the shirt information, but still believed Rook would be safer for her. The apology did not settle their disagreement.

That evening Maya signed a six-day reading agreement at one piece per day worked, with covered travel, meals, lodging, and return passage, freedom to stop, and no service bond or training obligation. She secured access to Elena's entry and departure records before work and the right to retain translation copies. The keeper witnessed. Vey confirmed that this arrangement would not earn Tolliver his finding fee. Maya spent two bits on supper, returned Tolliver's four untouched bits, and ended with seven personal bits. Alone, with reserve recovered to two out of four, she wrote a question beneath Elena's name asking whether Elena had found a way back, then prepared to leave.

## Chapter 10

The next morning Maya packed her possessions into a repaired flour sack, paid one bit for breakfast, and told Ordway she was leaving. Ordway reviewed the agreement, offered practical help through a paid carrier if Rook disputed it, and supplied food that included bread from Hester. Maya registered her departure with Bertran, who marked her provisional slip. She joined a two-cart convoy with Vey, Tolliver, the carter Nessa, another driver, and a young guard named Sella. Tolliver disclosed a separate two-piece road-escort payment, distinguishing it from the forfeited finding fee.

During the journey, Tolliver named his run as Tracker and described briefly following trails beyond visible signs, subject to weather and other limits; he could not track Elena after four years. Sella, who knew him from earlier work, punctured his competence with a story about following a goat. She described her own Warder run and three years of caravan work since eighteen. Her matter-of-fact humor gave Maya a peer relationship outside the fraught arrangements with Tolliver and Vey. Nessa displayed Carter, level fifteen, and Sella Guard, level twelve, reinforcing the distinction between a public label and a run.

At a watering stop, Sella taught Maya shield position and bracing. A strength burst cost one reserve point and demonstrated that strength alone did not prevent lateral displacement. Sella then demonstrated Brace, a Warder ability costing one point for up to three breaths while a supported alignment held. She warned Maya against choosing a run merely because a spare shield was available. Maya's progress increased from one to two out of fourteen during the lesson; her reserve, full that morning, fell to three out of four. The moment also forced her to compare practical defense with the mobile, magical swordplay she had imagined.

Near the day's wayhouse, Tolliver discovered a damaged grille at a stone crossing. Two reed-jacks attacked as the convoy attempted to withdraw. While Tolliver and Sella protected the horse and dealt with the first animal, the second climbed at Maya from the cart's rear. She freed the spare shield, protected her sack, and braced against the cart. Recognizing the taught pattern, she deliberately accepted the offered Warder run and spent a point on Brace. Vey tried to help with a knife, but Maya flinched and broke her first hold. A second point held the shield long enough for Sella to kill the creature. Maya survived without a bite but reopened her knuckles and acquired wrist and shoulder soreness. She had not defeated the animal alone.

At the wayhouse Maya confirmed level two, progress six out of fourteen, reserve one out of four, and Warder with Brace accessible. Sella urged further practice rather than treating the choice as mastery. Maya initiated a tentative touch and then held Tolliver's hand; the contact indicated growing willingness without resolving their conflict or becoming sexual. Her possessions remained intact despite tooth holes in the sack, and six personal bits remained.

After the covered evening meal, Vey offered a notebook tracing for voluntary inspection, acknowledging that paid work need not begin before Maya saw the promised records. English notes distinguished two gates, warned that the structure was not a drain, and advised against sending anyone through until a missing rope was understood. When Maya pressed him about the south sluice, Vey said two people had gone below and only one returned: Elena. The chapter ended on that unverified but consequential disclosure, still one travel day short of Rook House.

## Chapter 11

Still in the wayhouse common room, Maya pressed Vey to name the person who had not returned from the south sluice. He identified Iven Orrel, a sluicewright and the brother of Rook's keeper of records, Mara. Vey distinguished his own experience from Elena's account: he had arrived after Elena emerged wet and with injured hands, demanding that the mechanism be returned to a position that would let her search for Iven. Vey had supported the refusal. He described subsequent searches of the water and banks that recovered nothing, and said Elena left eleven days after the accident. He admitted withholding the incident because he feared Maya would refuse the journey before seeing the writing. He also admitted that Dain would tell Maya the truth the next day and would not obey an instruction to conceal it. His admission therefore remained partly self-protective.

Afterward, Sella helped bandage Maya's reopened knuckles and explained the dangers of using Brace against a rope. In Sella's account, the ability could work without a shield but would not strengthen skin, supports, or materials; a successful hold could still cause injury. Maya slept beside Sella on the shared platform, grieving for home and wondering how much information Liam could give anyone searching for her. The next morning her reserve had recovered to three out of four. During the remaining journey she practiced recognizing a failed stance and resetting against a wall without spending reserve. The convoy then reached Rook, a working settlement of mills, yards, dwellings, and waterworks rather than an isolated grand residence.

Mara met Maya in the records room. They compared both copies of the reading agreement and found them consistent, provisionally setting the next day as the first paid workday. Before any paid reading began, Mara showed Elena's entry and departure records. The entry recorded an adult at level one with no recorded origin, found on the western towpath after rain, declaring Missouri and admitted to a paid survey trial. Mara said she had witnessed Elena's English signature and remembered Elena being upset by her ability to read local script. The departure recorded level nine, settled wages, no continuing service claim, and departure by the west gate with a pack and staff. Mara calculated Elena's stay as eight months and twenty-six days, ending four years and slightly under two months earlier; those local dates did not establish a correspondence with Earth time.

The retained-materials record exposed an omission in Vey's story. Elena's chest remained because Rook had refused to release disputed survey materials, not because she had freely abandoned all of them. Her clothes and most personal belongings had been released, while the notebook, plans, tools, and some foreign objects remained under an unresolved ownership claim. Maya challenged the retention of Elena's identification and demanded that her claim remain attached to it.

The closure record bore Vey's signature. Dain's witness statement described a failed retaining tooth and unintended movement of the inner wheel; Mara said no replacement had been fitted. Elena's signed statement described a dry chamber, stairs beyond an arch, Iven crossing the sill, changing light, rising water, and recovery of a shorter line. She had not seen him fall or found a body and had objected to calling him drowned. Mara admitted crossing through Elena's report of lights without flame because she distrusted it, although she left the text legible. Finally Maya found an English scrap stating that Iven had still been pulling his end. Asked by Mara what it said, Maya read it aloud, newly aware of the responsibility her ability carried for someone else's grief.

## Chapter 12

Mara asked Maya to repeat Elena's six-word note. She said Elena had handed it to her when signing the statement, and admitted she had not examined it closely then. Maya resisted turning the message into proof that Iven survived. She copied the records, including the crossed-out sentence and the English scrap, and learned from Mara that Iven had two sons. The disclosure made care with uncertain information an obligation toward a family rather than an abstract rule.

Maya settled into a boltable room above the west kitchen, repaired her sack, and ate with the workers. Tolliver reported receiving his two-piece escort payment and said Sella had been paid too. He had accepted two days of channel-survey work with Dain, beginning the day after the following day, and disclosed the arrangement without making Maya responsible for his choice to remain. The next morning Maya explicitly began her six-day reading term. Vey returned Elena's card to Mara's recorded custody, and Mara presented the thirty-six-written-page notebook and associated small items.

The notebook mixed technical survey work with ordinary evidence of Elena's life, including wages, shopping, and attempts to explain coffee. Maya's ability to read English did not make her an engineer. Dain joined the work to interpret waterworks terminology and distinguish measurements from hypotheses. The original tracing and adjoining notes recorded different water conditions and the claim that changing the setting changed the source without identifying its location. Dain remembered removing unusual pale grit but warned that this did not establish its origin. A further annotation warned that closing the upstream gate did not remove the secondary inlet's load and that the inner wheel needed locking before a bypass test.

Recognizing an immediate problem, Dain hurried to a test already underway. The retaining tooth was still absent, and the crew had assumed upstream closure relieved the load. A trembling external indicator and subsequent impact showed unexpected movement while the bypass was drawing water. Dain directed the shutdown; Sella cleared five workers from the lower towpath while Maya managed the rope boundary without spending reserve. Nobody entered the closed lower works. The event established a practical hazard, not a verified portal or destination.

Steward Anja Pell suspended the test and required the warning in writing. She also explained that a crew was due in five days to remove the inner wheel and spindle so the bypass could become permanent drainage for repeatedly flooded fields. Maya wanted the mechanism preserved as a possible route home, while Pell required evidence sufficient to justify that choice. Dain secured a prohibition on cutting until he understood the unexpected load; the crew's arrival remained scheduled. Pell invited an item-specific review of Elena's identification claim, and Maya submitted one without receiving the card. Dain later reported intact visible fittings, an untouched access lock, and a small change in the inner-wheel indicator's position.

That evening Maya received her first piece, bringing her money to one piece and six bits. Her measure showed level two, progress eight out of fourteen, reserve four out of four, and Warder; the exact source of the two new progress points remained unknown. Tolliver explained the revised outer-bank work planned for the next day. Tolliver resented that Vey had obtained six days of her time when she had refused his advice. Maya challenged his sale of information, then initiated their first kisses while the disagreement remained unresolved. The mutual attraction became physically immediate without settling his accountability; Maya chose to retire alone. In her room she returned to Elena's notes, finding a proposal to use a mirror to see beyond the opening before crossing. She ended by planning to ask Dain what would make a safe observation possible, with no authorization yet obtained for entry or mechanism operation.

## Chapter 13

On the morning of Maya's second reading day, Dain explained the south works using a diagram: the wheel stood around a bend from the chamber and arch, while a separate upper gallery had an inspection sleeve through the chamber wall. A mirror might permit observation without entering the prohibited lower service passage. He agreed to inspect the upper access after the outer-bank survey, without promising entry or operation. Tolliver's affectionate greeting continued their newly physical relationship while both retained their own work.

Maya and Mara prioritized the notebook's works entries and numbered its thirty-six written pages with loose slips. They found that Elena had already lost a mirror through a failed hinged-rod experiment. Her proposed two-mirror alternative had no recorded result. Another entry remembered an unnamed person asking whether the light meant morning, which Maya suspected referred to Iven without adding his name to the translation. More consequentially, Elena recorded different water at the same wheel mark on separate days. That observation complicated the assumption that restoring a setting could reliably restore a destination, although it did not establish why the water differed. Maya also translated associated sample labels while keeping questions and missing samples explicit.

During a meal break at the closure boundary, Sella taught Maya to end Brace deliberately before its third breath and step away from the support.
