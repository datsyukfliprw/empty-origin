CHAPTER TWENTY-TWO

Rusk leaves before I wake. Her rig goes with her. The yard looks enormous without the shear legs.

Harra’s purchased block hangs beside the workroom door, small enough to carry with two hands and ugly enough that I trust it immediately.

The hook has no keeper. Harra has made one.

“Better than hers?” I ask.

“Mine closes.”

“That wasn’t the question.”

“It was the answer.”

The lower service door stands open behind her. For the first time. I forget the hook.

---

The stair smells like wet iron.

Not rot. Not mold. Iron and old water and the dust that comes off stone when nobody walks on it for years.

Dain and Harra have already marked the damaged steps, the missing rail, and the wheel recess above the chamber. Nobody crossed the boundary while the interval changed. That is why I am allowed to go. Allowed is doing work here.

The mender examines my ankle again, makes me stand, makes me rise onto both toes, then tells me I may manage indoor stairs with a handrail and another person present.

“Not carrying weight.”

“I weigh something.”

“She means equipment,” Sella says.

“I know what she means.”

“Then why are you like this?”

The mender answers before I can.

“Nineteen.”

Sella looks delighted. I decide not to respect either of them.

---

We establish the abort conditions at the kitchen table.

Not in the chamber, not while afraid, and not after somebody has already put a boot where they shouldn’t.

Dain writes them large.

If the boundary contracts early, the outlet drops past threshold, an anchor moves, visual contact is lost, or either Harra or Iven calls stop, we withdraw. No body or hand crosses the edge. Nobody improvises because an object is “almost there.”

Mara reads the list. Then she adds one. NO SECOND TRY TODAY AFTER EQUIPMENT LOSS. Dain looks at her.

“What?”

“You will want one.”

He thinks about it. Then writes it down. I love her a little.

---

My job is not the rope, the block, or stepping through a magical murder-slice because I happen to have a class named Warder.

My job is the test board. Dain has taken yesterday’s interval observations and made them physical. A narrow plank, marked with three zones. Green: rising pressure above the first observed threshold, boundary widening. Yellow: uncertain.

Red: falling below the lowest observed safe continuation mark.

The colors are not a law. They are reminders that uncertainty exists even when people are excited.

I stand at the wheel recess with the board, close enough to hear Oret’s runner call the outlet marks from above, far enough from the chamber that nobody can mistake my presence for permission to approach the edge.

Sella stands with me. Tolliver is at the outside stair relay. Mara remains at the upper glass with Iven. Harra and Dain are below.

Renn is the extra body whose entire job is to pull Dain backward if Dain becomes stupid.

Dain objected to this description. Renn did not.

---

The wheel recess is smaller than I imagined, a stone pocket beside the stair half hidden behind a heavy vertical spindle. The wheel itself has been restrained at an angle by the clamp above. One tooth in the old retaining gear is missing, leaving a black gap in the ring.

Nobody touches it. A chalk line across the floor says: DO NOT TOUCH. Somebody has added beneath it: MAYA. I look at Sella.

“That one was Tolliver.”

“How do you know?”

“His letters lean.”

“Of course you know that.”

“I have interests.”

She smiles. The runner calls from the stair above.

“Outlet rising.”

I move the wooden marker to yellow. Harra’s voice comes up from the chamber.

“Width increasing.”

Green. My heart begins to beat harder.

Not because I am doing anything. Because somewhere below, the world is opening wider.

---

The test object is a narrow cross-braced ash frame, not a bridge. Harra says this often enough that even my brain stops trying to promote it.

We have measured Iven’s landing.

He has cleared a place beside the gray door and braced one end of his bed-frame strip against the wall.

The plan sounds sane when written down. Harra advances the frame from our side; Iven catches the far nose and lowers it onto his landing, leaving no line tensioned across the boundary. If it can rest independently on both structures, we learn something useful about geometry and load.

If not, we lose a frame. That was easier to say at the table.

---

“Green,” I call.

Dain repeats it below. The chain begins moving. One link. Pause. Another. Harra calls measurements.

Sella repeats them beside me, not because I cannot hear but because we decided every critical number gets heard twice.

“Two hands extension.”

“Two hands.”

“Three.”

“Three.”

The runner arrives breathless.

“Outlet still rising.”

Green. From above, Mara’s bell rings once. Iven sees the nose. The chain stops. Dain calls, “Far contact beginning.” I stare at the board. Do not imagine the frame, Iven’s hands, or what would happen to fingers. I fail at all three.

“Outlet steady!”

Yellow. The boundary is still wide. Harra calls, “Holding.” Mara’s bell rings twice. Iven has the frame. A long pause. Then Harra says, “Far end down.” Nobody cheers. We have learned. That is enough.

Dain orders the near end lowered toward the Rook support block. The frame settles. A faint vibration comes up through the floor. Sella feels it too. Her hand closes around my wrist.

“Anchor?”

I call. Harra answers, “Sound.” Renn repeats, “Sound.”

For one impossible second, the frame is supported on both sides. Not by the boundary. By stone.

A thing exists across the connected space. Nothing is tied through. Nothing is moving. I understand why every stupid part of me wants to step onto it.

---

The runner says, “Outlet falling.” Yellow. We knew it would. That is the test. Harra begins retrieval. The frame must come back before the lower threshold. Iven lifts his end with the bed strip. Near side rises. Chain takes weight. One link.

Two. The runner calls again.

“Falling faster.”

Red.

“Withdraw,” I say.

Dain repeats it instantly. The chain moves. Then stops. Not slowly. Stops. Harra swears.

“Far nose caught.”

My mouth goes dry.

“Release it,” Dain says.

“Iven’s trying.”

Mara’s bell from above. One. Then one again. He sees the problem. The frame has dropped between two stones on the far landing. Not deep. Deep enough. Harra pulls. Dain says, “No.” The chain goes slack.

Good. No holding harder. We learned that already. Harra feeds an inch back. Mara’s bell rings twice. Iven has lifted.

“Take,” Harra says.

Chain. One link. The runner arrives.

“Below mark!”

Red was already red. Now it means something else.

“Abort,” I say.

Dain says it before the word finishes leaving my mouth. Harra hauls. The frame moves. Half a hand. Then the chamber makes a sound I have never heard before. Not stone. Not metal.

A flat crack, like a book slammed shut by a giant. The chain jumps. Harra shouts. Sella is already moving down the stair. I grab her sleeve.

“No.”

Her whole body strains against my hand for half a second. Then she stops. We do not break our rule because the sound was frightening. Below, Renn calls, “They’re clear!” I breathe. Dain shouts up, “Frame lost. Line clear.”

“People?”

“Clear.”

I let go of Sella. She presses the heel of her hand to her mouth.

---

The boundary contracts through the frame. We do not see it happen from the recess. Mara does. Iven does.

Later, when the interval widens enough to inspect from the glass, the result is obvious.

The near half of the ash frame lies in our chamber. The far half sits on Iven’s landing.

One crossbrace is gone entirely. Either cut into a piece too small to see or thrown somewhere outside the view.

The break is diagonal. Clean through one sidepiece. Splintered through the other where the load twisted at the last instant.

The guide line never crossed the edge. Harra had detached it from the frame before the independent support test, exactly as planned.

We lost the frame. Not the block, a hand, or a person. I repeat this until it begins to sound true.

---

Mara reaches the chamber before I do. I am slow on the stairs. Nobody comments.

Dain is sitting on the floor with his back against the wall, staring at the cut ash.

Renn stands over him.

“You didn’t have to drag him?” I ask.

“Didn’t get the pleasure.”

Dain gives us both a tired look. Harra is examining the remaining metal pin.

“It wasn’t timing alone.”

I wait. She points to the far end drawing.

“The nose caught because the support face isn’t level. Iven’s landing pitches toward the door.”

“The cup would have told us.”

Dain looks up. I blink. He starts laughing. Not a lot. Enough.

“The dented cup,” he says.

“You mocked my cup.”

“I mocked nothing. Harra mocked the cup.”

Harra does not look up.

“I stand by it.”

The laugh hurts less than the silence. Then Harra taps the pin.

“And this was too proud.”

The pin that should have seated into the receiving notch extends farther than the drawing allowed. Under no load, that gave us tolerance. Under withdrawal, it hooked the stone edge.

Known weak point. Fixable. But not today. Mara’s rule. No second try after loss.

Dain looks as though he wants to eat the paper it is written on.

He does not argue.

---

Iven sends a message through the upper glass. FRAME HALF HERE. THREE STEPS SOUND. DOOR JAMB SOUND. I CUT PIN FREE LATER? Harra answers: NO. LEAVE EVERYTHING UNTIL NEXT CONTACT. He writes: YES, HARra. His local spelling of her name is terrible.

She pretends not to be pleased. Then he adds another line. TELL MAYA NOT EARTH IS NOT NOTHING. Mara stops reading. I look at the slate.

“Did he write that?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

She gives me a look so similar to my mother’s that for one awful instant I hate her. Then it passes.

Iven writes again. FALLOW REACH EXISTS. YOU FOUND WHERE I AM. I sit on the gallery bench because my ankle has begun to throb. He is right. I hate that too.

---

The mender makes me put my foot up when we return to the kitchen.

I tell her the stair was approved. She says nothing about the number of trips I made. This is how I know I am in trouble. Sella drops into the chair beside me. Her hands are still shaking slightly. I take one.

“You stopped.”

“So did you.”

“I had your sleeve.”

“You had one hand.”

“That was enough.”

She looks at me.

“You wanted to go down.”

“Yes.”

“So did I.”

“Yes.”

Neither of us tries to make a virtue out of wanting to go down.

---

Tolliver returns from the outer relay after the equipment is inventoried. He sets a folded page beside me.

“What?”

“Rusk.”

I open it. A receipt. Not for us. For the west-road offer. Declined.

At the bottom he has written the missing man’s name and the name of the daughter from years ago.

Elian Vos. Mera Vos. No explanation. Just the two names.

“You’re giving me this?”

“I don’t want to keep making you ask what I am hiding.”

The sentence lands carefully. Not polished, not enough to erase anything. Real anyway. I fold the page.

“Thank you.”

He nods. Then he looks at my ankle.

“You overdid the stairs.”

“I have been medically cleared to overdo a limited number of stairs.”

“That isn’t a thing.”

“Don’t start.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

I look at him.

He nods toward the stool by the hearth. “I was going to ask if you wanted that under your foot.”

The difference is small enough to miss. I don’t.

“Yes,” I say.

He brings me the stool.

“You sound like Sella.”

From the table behind him, Sella says, “He should be more offended.” Tolliver looks over his shoulder. She holds his gaze. For once, nobody reaches for a fight.

---

The house extends all three of our work by one more day. Not because Pell has found money.

Because the drainage is now operating and three tenants pay part of what they owe in stored grain, enough to free a small works purse without touching seed.

Pell explains every bit of it. I appreciate that more than I want to admit. Maya: eight bits. Sella: twelve. Tolliver: ten. One day. Flat work for me unless the mender changes it.

Harra gets materials for a second frame, shorter pin, adjustable far shoe. No new rescue attempt tomorrow. First, we inspect the far half when the boundary permits. Then load-test the near anchor without spanning. Then decide.

The clerk pays today’s eight bits before I sign for tomorrow. The money is becoming less important than the paper. That frightens me differently.

---

At night, I open the measure.

**[MAYA SZCZEPANIAK, LV. 3]**

**[PROGRESS: 15 / 18]**

**[RESERVE: 5 / 5]**

**[RUN: WARDER]**

Fifteen. Three to go. I sit with that for a while.

The class name no longer feels like a label someone put on me while I was unconscious.

Warder.

Hold what can be held. Release what should not be. Know the difference before the world teaches it with teeth.

That thought is dangerously close to becoming a lesson. I stop before I ruin it. My ankle aches. The frame is in two worlds that are really one. Iven is still trapped.

Tomorrow we do not rescue him. We learn why we failed. For once, that does not feel like waiting.
